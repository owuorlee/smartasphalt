How it works · the plain-language version

A road that carries its own repair kit.

No engineering background needed. If you want the technical version, see For engineers.

In one sentence: Smart Asphalt is road surface made partly from waste plastic and cooking oil, with microscopic oil capsules inside that help cracks close on their own when the sun warms the road.

First, what is a road made of?

Stones, held together by black glue.

The black surface of a road is asphalt: about 95% crushed stone and sand, glued together by about 5% bitumen, a sticky petroleum product. The glue is what fails. Over the years, sun and air make it hard and brittle, like old rubber. Heavy trucks bend the road thousands of times a day. Eventually a hairline crack opens, rain gets in, and the crack becomes a pothole.

Today’s fix is to wait for the pothole, then patch it or resurface the road. Smart Asphalt’s idea is to repair the crack while it is still tiny.

What goes into Smart Asphalt

Three changes to normal asphalt.

  1. Recycled plastic in the glue

    Plastic film (bags, wrappers) and bottle caps are cleaned, shredded and melted into the bitumen. Like reinforcement, it makes the glue stiffer so the road resists the ruts that heavy wheels leave. India has already built about 40,000 km of rural road using waste plastic. Source T4Source T5

  2. Microscopic repair capsules

    Billions of tiny beads, thinner than a human hair, are mixed through the road. Each has a shell made from seaweed (alginate) and a core of plant oil made from used cooking oil and castor oil. The oil is a rejuvenator: it softens old, hardened bitumen. Source T1Source T8

  3. Less imported bitumen

    Because plastic and plant oil take the place of part of the bitumen, each tonne of road needs less of it: about 10–15% less in our design. Source M1

How the road heals

Four steps, powered by the sun.

1

A crack starts

Heavy traffic and the sun slowly age the road. A hairline crack forms, the first step towards a pothole.

2

Capsules break

Tiny capsules of plant oil are mixed all through the road. When the crack reaches them, they break open.

3

Oil flows in

The oil seeps into the crack and softens the old, brittle road glue (bitumen) on both sides.

4

The sun seals it

Kenya’s sun warms the road. The softened edges flow together and the crack closes, before it becomes a pothole.

Why the sun matters: warm bitumen flows more easily. Studies report that healing increases significantly above about 40°C, and Kenyan road surfaces regularly get that hot. Kenya’s heat, usually a road’s enemy, becomes the repair engine. Source T2Source P12

Common questions, quickly

What people usually ask next.

Do the capsules break when the road is made?

Asphalt is mixed hot, at about 150–170°C. Researchers at TU Delft showed similar seaweed capsules survive this. Ours are designed to be added at the cooler end of mixing, with a strengthened shell. Testing this is our first lab step. Full answer

Can the road heal forever?

No. Each capsule works once. There are millions in every square metre, so a road can heal many small cracks over its life, but eventually the supply runs out and normal maintenance resumes.

Is putting plastic in roads safe?

The plastic is melted into the glue, not left as loose pieces. Studies so far suggest it sheds far fewer particles than tyres do, but this is not fully settled, so our pilot measures it. Full answer

Does it fix existing potholes?

No. It is a new road surface. It prevents small cracks from becoming potholes; it cannot fill a pothole that already exists, and it cannot stop the ground under a road from moving.
