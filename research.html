The research

Four chapters of research, explained simply.

We did four rounds of research. Each chapter below says what we asked, what we found, and how sure we are. The full reports are one click away. They are the verified editions: the headline facts were checked against public sources.

"How sure we are" is the part to read carefully. Our own estimates are never presented as facts.

1

Can a road heal itself?

The question
Can tiny capsules of plant oil, made from materials available in Kenya, repair cracks in asphalt?
What we found
The capsule idea is real, published science: researchers at TU Delft and elsewhere made calcium-alginate capsules, mixed them into asphalt, and saw healing in the lab. We worked out a version using seaweed alginate shells and a core of cooking oil, castor oil and sugarcane-waste oil, plus a way to make it at pilot scale. Kenya's warm roads should help, since heating above 40°C speeds healing in published studies.
How sure we are
Sure: capsules survive asphalt production. Not sure: how much healing to expect. Published results range from a few percent to about 80%, and our own '70 to 85%' was an estimate, not a published figure. No test has been done with our exact recipe.
Key numbers
Capsules survive asphalt mixing (verified) Evidence T1 · healing amount varies widely Evidence T2
Technical detail

Contents: shell materials (calcium alginate with nano-silica), core oils (waste cooking oil, castor oil), healing mechanism, a manufacturing route from laboratory to 100 kg a day, Kenya's climate zones, and a cost analysis. Evidence P7Evidence T2

What does Kenya's bitumen dependence cost?

The question
How much does Kenya spend on imported bitumen, how exposed is the supply, and which of eight possible solutions look most promising?
What we found
Kenya imported USD 27.5 million of bitumen in 2023 and USD 20.1 million in 2024, mostly from Gulf-region suppliers. We compared eight ways to reduce that: a refinery, plastic-modified bitumen, lignin bio-bitumen, recycling old asphalt (RAP), self-healing capsules, crumb rubber, a regional hub, and a fully synthetic binder. Recycling old asphalt and plastic-modified bitumen looked like the quickest, cheapest wins; capsules a good second step.
How sure we are
Sure: the import data (UN Comtrade). Not sure: the scoring of the eight options and the ten-year savings. Those are our judgement and models.
Key numbers
USD 20–28 million a year Evidence P1Evidence P2 · Gulf-region share 72% to 91% Evidence P3 · shilling stable at about KES 129 per USD Evidence P6
Technical detail

Contents: the cost of imports, supply-chain risk, currency and port costs, eight solutions with financial analysis, a comparison matrix, a hybrid strategy and policy recommendations. Macro-economic projections in this report are our own estimates and were not independently verified.

3

What are the alternatives to pure bitumen?

The question
What is the full range of road binders, from modified bitumen to bitumen-free roads, and which fit Kenya?
What we found
We laid out ten technologies as a 'ladder': polymer-modified bitumen, recycled plastic, crumb rubber, natural rubber, warm-mix asphalt, sulphur-extended asphalt, bio-rejuvenators, lignin bio-binder, a fully synthetic binder and geopolymer concrete. Imported polymers or sulphur only swap one import for another. The best path for Kenya is to stack local options: plastic, cooking oil, recycled asphalt and warm-mix first.
How sure we are
Sure: each technology exists in published research or in use. Not sure: the exact bitumen savings and costs for Kenya. Those are our estimates. The fully synthetic binder is not proven for heavy traffic anywhere.
Key numbers
Warm-mix saves about 20–22% plant fuel Evidence T9 · plastic roads exist at scale in India Evidence T4 · cooking oil rejuvenates old bitumen Evidence T8
Technical detail

Contents: Kenya's current binder practice, ten technologies each with a Kenya verdict, a master comparison, technology stacking, pilot projects by road class and ten recommendations. Evidence W2Evidence W3Evidence W4

4

Can we put it all together?

The question
Can a plastic-modified, self-healing, lower-bitumen binder be designed for Kenya's climates, and what would it take to make and test it?
What we found
We designed 'Smart Asphalt': a plastic backbone, repair capsules and less bitumen, with three zone-specific formulas (hot coast, Nairobi highlands, black-cotton soil), a manufacturing workflow, a risk analysis and a five-year roadmap that begins with laboratory tests and 500 m pilot roads.
How sure we are
Sure: the ingredients exist and several parts are documented. Not sure: every performance, cost and life-cycle figure. They are our own model: about 10-15% less bitumen, about double the design life, about 1.5% higher construction cost and 30-37% lower life-cycle cost. None has been measured.
Key numbers
All headline performance numbers are design targets Evidence M1Evidence M2Evidence M3 · stage: laboratory (TRL 4-5), no Kenyan trial yet
Technical detail

Contents: the plastic backbone, bio-oil capsules, the hybrid blending protocol and compatibility control, zone-specific engineering, mix design, a manufacturing plant layout, performance prediction, economics and business models, risk analysis, and three master formulation data sheets. The report contains exact formulations. Evidence M4Evidence M5
