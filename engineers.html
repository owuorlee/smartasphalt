System overview

A hot-mix (or warm-mix) asphalt in which the conventional penetration-grade bitumen binder is replaced by a hybrid binder with three functional phases:

PhaseMaterialFunctionShare (general)
Base binderPenetration-grade bitumen (60/70 or 80/100 by zone)Cohesion, aggregate adhesionMajority of the binder
Structural modifierRecycled LDPE/PP (film, caps), wet process, with a maleic-anhydride-grafted PE compatibiliserHigh-temperature stiffness, rutting resistanceA few percent of binder mass, within the 3–8% range reported as effective Source T5Source T6
Healing agentEncapsulated bio-rejuvenator in calcium-alginate / nano-silica shellsCrack-triggered rejuvenation and healingA few percent of binder mass

Design aim: 10–15% less virgin bitumen per tonne of mix, with higher rutting resistance and autonomous micro-crack healing. Source M1

Plastic-modified binder

  • Feedstock: post-consumer LDPE film and PP, sorted (no PVC, no PET), washed, dried and shredded to small flakes; melt-flow index checked per batch.
  • Wet process: plastic dispersed into hot bitumen with a high-shear mixer; compatibiliser added to bridge the non-polar polymer and the polar asphaltene fraction and prevent phase separation. Source T6
  • Acceptance: storage stability (ASTM D7173 tube test), softening point (ASTM D36), penetration (ASTM D5), elastic recovery (ASTM D6084), rotational viscosity (ASTM D4402).

Capsule design

  • Shell: calcium alginate formed by ionic gelation, reinforced with nano-SiO2 for stiffness and thermal stability; a brittle shell is preferred so it fractures cleanly at a crack tip.
  • Core: a bio-rejuvenator blend of pre-treated waste cooking oil (filtered, dehydrated, acid value controlled) and castor oil; upgraded bagasse bio-oil is a later option. The oils replenish the maltene fraction of aged bitumen. Source T8
  • Size: tens of micrometres (design target). Published TU Delft capsules were millimetre-sized, so ours are a smaller design to be validated. Source T1
  • Scale-up route: emulsification with internal gelation (avoids nozzle clogging), fluid-bed drying.
  • QC: size distribution (laser diffraction), encapsulation efficiency, thermogravimetric analysis, micro-compression strength, SEM.

Production sequence

  1. Disperse plastic into hot bitumen at high shear; add compatibiliser.
  2. Reduce binder temperature and switch to low shear before adding capsules, to limit thermal and mechanical damage.
  3. Mix the binder with heated aggregate in a standard batch or drum plant; warm-mix additives can lower temperatures by 20–40°C, which saves fuel (measured plant savings about 20–22%) and protects capsules. Source T9
  4. Pave and compact with standard equipment.

Order of operations matters: capsules must never see high-shear blending.

Healing mechanism

Under cyclic loading, micro-cracks initiate at stress concentrations (aggregate interfaces, voids). The stress intensity at a propagating crack tip greatly exceeds bulk tyre-contact stress (about 0.1–0.7 MPa), so a correctly tuned shell fractures preferentially at the crack front. Released oil wets the crack faces by capillarity and diffuses into the aged binder, lowering its viscosity and asphaltene-to-maltene ratio so the faces can flow and re-bond. Healing is diffusion-controlled and strongly temperature-dependent: published work reports a significant increase above about 40°C, and Kenya’s maximum pavement design temperature is about 64°C. Source T2Source T3Source P12

Each capsule is single-use; healing capacity per zone depends on capsule density and declines over successive cracking events.

Validation plan and test methods

QuestionTests
Do capsules survive production?Thermogravimetric analysis of capsules; capsule counts in mixed and compacted specimens by microscopy and X-ray CT
How much does it heal?Fatigue–rest–fatigue tests (beam or semi-circular bending) at controlled temperatures; DSR healing tests on binder
Is the binder stable and strong?Storage stability, softening point, penetration, DSR and MSCR (rutting), BBR (low temperature)
Is the mix durable?Marshall design, indirect tensile strength, tensile strength ratio (moisture), Hamburg wheel tracking
Is it safe for the environment?Fume sampling (VOCs, PAHs) during mixing; leaching tests; micro- and nanoplastic analysis of wear debris and runoff Source E1Source E2Source E3
Does it work in the field?Truck-yard pilot with a control section: embedded temperature and strain sensors, crack mapping, deflection testing, runoff sampling

Open engineering questions

  • Capsule survival at our smaller size, and selectivity: rupture at cracks versus under repeated heavy load. Source T3
  • Healing magnitude for our formulation under Kenyan temperature cycles. Source T2
  • Interaction of rejuvenator oil with the plastic-modified binder: risk of over-softening and reduced rutting resistance.
  • Long-term ageing, moisture damage, and recyclability of the hybrid binder as RAP.
  • Emissions, leaching and micro/nanoplastic release under field conditions. Source E1Source E2Source E3
  • Reconciling per-kilometre material quantities across our reports. Source M8
