## 2. COMPONENT 1: STRUCTURAL BACKBONE — RECYCLED PLASTIC INTEGRATION

### 2.1 Why Recycled Plastic?

Recycled plastic polymers — specifically LDPE (Low-Density Polyethylene) and PP (Polypropylene) — serve as the structural modifier in Smart Asphalt. When properly dispersed in hot bitumen via high-shear milling, they:

1. **Swell by absorbing maltene fractions** from the bitumen, creating a polymer-rich gel network
2. **Increase viscosity and stiffness** at high service temperatures → reduces rutting
3. **Improve elastic recovery** → better fatigue performance under repeated loading
4. **Raise softening point** by 10–20°C → prevents binder bleeding in Mombasa-type heat

### 2.2 Kenya's Plastic Feedstock

| Plastic Type | Melting Point | Kenya Availability | Suitability |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **LDPE** (shopping bags, packaging film) | 105–115°C | ★★★★★ — dominant waste type (banned bags stockpiled) | **BEST** — low melting point, easy dispersion |
| **PP** (bottle caps, containers, furniture) | 130–170°C | ★★★★☆ — abundant in urban waste | **EXCELLENT** — higher stiffness contribution |
| **HDPE** (jerry cans, pipes) | 125–135°C | ★★★★☆ — significant volumes from industrial waste | **GOOD** — balanced properties |
| **PET** (drink bottles) | 250–260°C | ★★★★★ — most visible waste type | **POOR for wet method** — too high melting point, incompatible |
| **PS** (styrofoam, cups) | 100–120°C | ★★★☆☆ — moderate volumes | **ACCEPTABLE** — dissolves well, but may generate fumes |

> [!WARNING]
> **PET (polyethylene terephthalate) is NOT suitable for the wet method.** Its melting point (250°C+) far exceeds safe bitumen mixing temperatures. PET-based approaches require the dry method (coating aggregates), which is a different process. Smart Asphalt uses LDPE and PP exclusively.

### 2.3 The Wet Method: Detailed Protocol

**The Wet Method** blends shredded plastic directly into hot bitumen before adding aggregates.

```
SMART ASPHALT WET METHOD — PLASTIC INTEGRATION PROTOCOL

STEP 1: PLASTIC PREPARATION
├── Source: Collected LDPE/PP from Nairobi/Mombasa waste streams
├── Sorting: Manual + automated NIR sorting (remove PET, PVC contaminants)
├── Washing: Hot water wash (60°C) to remove food residue, labels
├── Shredding: Industrial shredder → 2–5 mm flakes
├── Drying: Air-dry to <1% moisture content
└── Quality check: Melt Flow Index (MFI), contaminant levels

STEP 2: HIGH-SHEAR BLENDING
├── Heat base bitumen (Pen 80/100) to 160–170°C in jacketed mixing vessel
├── Add shredded LDPE/PP at 6% by weight of bitumen
│   (Example: 60 kg plastic per 1,000 kg bitumen)
├── Engage high-shear mixer at 2,500–3,000 RPM
├── Blend for 60–90 minutes at 165°C (±5°C)
├── Monitor: Visual check for homogeneity (no visible flakes)
├── Add compatibilizer: PE-g-MA at 1.5–2.0% of plastic weight
│   (0.9–1.2 kg per 1,000 kg bitumen)
└── Continue mixing for 15 minutes after compatibilizer addition

STEP 3: QUALITY VERIFICATION
├── Storage stability test (ASTM D7173): Tube test, 163°C, 48 hrs
│   Acceptance: Softening point difference (top-bottom) ≤ 2.5°C
├── Softening point: Target 58–68°C (Ring & Ball, ASTM D36)
├── Penetration @ 25°C: Target 40–60 dmm (ASTM D5)
├── Elastic recovery @ 25°C: Target >60% (ASTM D6084)
└── Viscosity @ 135°C: Target 1.5–3.0 Pa·s (ASTM D4402)
```

### 2.4 The Role of PE-g-MA Compatibilizer

**Maleic anhydride-grafted polyethylene (PE-g-MA)** is the critical enabler that prevents the plastic-modified binder from separating during storage:

```
CHEMISTRY OF COMPATIBILIZATION:

Non-polar PE chain ——— [Maleic Anhydride Graft] ——— Polar bitumen components
     (plastic)              (bridging agent)              (asphaltenes)

PE-g-MA mechanism:
1. The polyethylene backbone integrates with the dispersed LDPE/PP phase
2. The maleic anhydride groups (-CO-O-CO-) react with:
   ├── Hydroxyl groups (-OH) in asphaltenes → ester bonds
   └── Amino groups (-NH₂) in bitumen polar fractions → imide bonds
3. Result: Chemical bridges between polymer and bitumen phases
4. Outcome: Homogeneous, storage-stable blend (no phase separation)

Dosage: 1.5–2.0% by weight of total plastic content
Cost: ~USD 2.50–4.00/kg (imported; no local production)
```

### 2.5 Optimal Plastic Dosage: The Evidence Base

| Plastic Content (% of binder) | Softening Point Increase | Marshall Stability | Storage Stability | Recommendation |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| 2% | +3–5°C | +8–12% | Good | Too low — minimal benefit |
| 4% | +8–12°C | +18–25% | Good | Acceptable minimum |
| **6%** | **+13–18°C** | **+25–40%** | **Good (with PE-g-MA)** | **OPTIMAL** |
| 8% | +18–22°C | +30–45% | Marginal | Approaching limit |
| 10% | +20–25°C | +35–50% | Poor (segregation risk) | Too high — stability issues |

## 3. COMPONENT 2: HEALING AGENT — LIGNIN-BASED BIO-OIL MICROCAPSULES

### 3.1 The Dual-Function Bio-Oil

The healing agent in Smart Asphalt performs two simultaneous functions:

1. **Rejuvenation:** Restores maltene-to-asphaltene ratio in aged binder surrounding cracks
2. **Lubrication:** Reduces local viscosity at crack faces, enabling them to flow together and bond

**Optimal Bio-Oil Formulation for Smart Asphalt:**

| Component | Proportion | Source in Kenya | Function |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Waste cooking oil (WCO)** | 60% | Nairobi/Mombasa HORECA sector | Primary rejuvenator; maltene replenishment |
| **Castor oil** | 20% | Machakos/Kitui smallholder farms | Low-temperature fluidity; hydroxyl-group compatibility |
| **Bagasse pyrolysis bio-oil** | 20% | Western Kenya sugar belt | Lignin-derived aromatics; aging retardation |

### 3.2 Bagasse Pyrolysis Bio-Oil: Extraction & Upgrading

**From Sugarcane Bagasse to Asphalt Healing Agent:**

```
LIGNIN BIO-OIL PRODUCTION PATHWAY

STAGE 1: FEEDSTOCK PREPARATION
├── Source: Sugarcane bagasse from Mumias, Nzoia, Chemelil mills
├── Volume available: ~2.4 million tonnes/year (about 1 million tonnes dumped or burned; the rest powers the mills)
├── Drying: Sun-dry to <10% moisture content
├── Milling: Hammer mill to <2 mm particle size
└── Storage: Covered, dry warehouse

STAGE 2: LIGNIN EXTRACTION (Soda Process — preferred for Kenya)
├── Reagent: 12–15% NaOH solution
├── Temperature: 160–180°C
├── Time: 2–4 hours in pressurised reactor
├── Output: Black liquor (dissolved lignin + hemicellulose + NaOH)
├── Precipitation: Add H₂SO₄ to pH 2.0 → lignin precipitates
├── Filtration: Vacuum filter → wet lignin cake
├── Washing: 3× distilled water wash
├── Drying: Spray dry or oven dry (105°C, 12 hrs)
└── Yield: ~25–35% of bagasse dry weight (lignin purity >85%)

STAGE 3: FAST PYROLYSIS (Lignin → Bio-Oil)
├── Reactor: Fluidized bed reactor
├── Temperature: 450–500°C (optimum for liquid yield)
├── Atmosphere: N₂ (oxygen-free)
├── Residence time: 1–3 seconds
├── Condensation: Rapid cooling of vapours → bio-oil liquid
├── Yield: 30–45% bio-oil, 25–35% biochar, 20–30% syngas
└── Bio-oil characteristics: Dark brown liquid, pH 2–3, viscosity 10–50 cSt

STAGE 4: BIO-OIL UPGRADING (Critical for Asphalt Compatibility)
├── Dehydration: Rotary evaporation, 80°C, vacuum → reduce moisture to <5%
├── Neutralisation: Add NaHCO₃ to raise pH to 5.0–6.0
├── Chemical stabilisation: Add MDI (2,4-diphenylmethane diisocyanate)
│   at 2–5% by weight → cross-links reactive phenolics
├── Filtration: Remove char fines via 10 µm membrane filter
├── Viscosity adjustment: Blend with WCO to target 30–60 mPa·s @ 25°C
└── Quality control: Acid value, moisture, viscosity, ash content

OUTPUT: "KE-BioRej" — Kenya Bio-Rejuvenator blend
├── Composition: 60% WCO + 20% castor oil + 20% upgraded bagasse bio-oil
├── Viscosity @ 25°C: 35–55 mPa·s
├── Acid value: <5.0 mg KOH/g
├── Moisture: <0.5%
└── Flash point: >180°C
```

## 4. COMPONENT 3: THE HYBRID BINDER — CHEMICAL SYNTHESIS & BLENDING PROTOCOL

### 4.1 The Master Blending Sequence

> [!CAUTION]
> **Order of operations is CRITICAL.** The three components must be combined in a specific sequence to prevent chemical incompatibility, premature capsule rupture, or polymer degradation.

```
SMART ASPHALT BINDER PRODUCTION — MASTER PROTOCOL

PHASE A: PLASTIC-MODIFIED BINDER PREPARATION (Duration: 2 hours)
═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. Heat base bitumen (Pen 80/100) to 165°C in jacketed vessel
2. Add shredded LDPE/PP (6% by binder weight) gradually over 15 min
3. Engage high-shear mixer at 2,500–3,000 RPM
4. Blend for 60 minutes at 165°C
5. Add PE-g-MA compatibiliser (1.5% of plastic weight)
6. Continue blending for 15 minutes
7. Reduce mixer speed to 500 RPM (gentle stirring)
8. Allow temperature to stabilise at 160°C
→ OUTPUT: Plastic-Modified Binder (PMB)

PHASE B: MICROCAPSULE INCORPORATION (Duration: 15 minutes)
═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
9. Cool PMB to 145–150°C (CRITICAL — reduces thermal stress on capsules)
10. Add pre-weighed microcapsules (3.5% by weight of total binder)
    ├── Disperse slowly via calibrated hopper/feeder
    └── Do NOT dump all at once (causes agglomeration)
11. Stir at LOW SHEAR (300–500 RPM) for 10–15 minutes
    └── High shear will rupture capsules prematurely!
12. Verify uniform dispersion (visual + sample microscopy)
→ OUTPUT: Smart Asphalt Binder (SAB) — ready for aggregate mixing

PHASE C: ASPHALT MIX PRODUCTION (Duration: standard HMA production)
═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
13. Heat aggregates to 160–170°C (standard HMA protocol)
14. Add SAB at optimum binder content (5.0–5.8% by mix weight)
15. Mix for 60–90 seconds in pugmill
16. Discharge, transport, pave, compact as per standard practice
→ OUTPUT: Smart Asphalt Pavement
```

### 4.2 Critical Compatibility Analysis

The hybrid system contains three chemically distinct phases that must coexist:

| Phase | Chemistry | Polarity | Compatibility Risk |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Bitumen** (residual) | Asphaltenes + maltenes + resins | Polar (asphaltenes) + Non-polar (maltenes) | — |
| **LDPE/PP** (structural) | Long-chain hydrocarbons, non-polar | Non-polar | ⚠️ Separates from polar asphaltenes |
| **Bio-oil** (in capsules) | Fatty acids + phenolic compounds + aromatics | Mixed polar | ⚠️ May over-soften bitumen if released in excess |

**Compatibility Management Strategy:**

```
COMPATIBILITY CONTROL MATRIX

RISK 1: Plastic-Bitumen Phase Separation
├── CONTROL: PE-g-MA compatibiliser (bridges polarity gap)
├── CONTROL: High-shear mixing (fine polymer dispersion, <10 µm domains)
└── VERIFICATION: Tube storage stability test (ASTM D7173)

RISK 2: Bio-Oil Over-Softening
├── CONTROL: Encapsulation (bio-oil is sequestered until crack trigger)
├── CONTROL: Limited capsule dosage (3.5% — provides healing without
│            excessive softening)
├── CONTROL: Blended bio-oil viscosity (higher than pure WCO alone)
└── VERIFICATION: DSR testing (complex modulus G* after simulated healing)

RISK 3: Capsule Premature Rupture
├── CONTROL: Lower mixing temperature during capsule addition (145–150°C)
├── CONTROL: Low-shear stirring only (300–500 RPM; NO high-shear)
├── CONTROL: Nano-SiO₂ reinforced shells (survival rate >85%)
└── VERIFICATION: Post-mixing microscopy (capsule integrity check)

RISK 4: Chemical Interaction Between Plastic & Bio-Oil
├── ANALYSIS: LDPE/PP and fatty acid-based bio-oils are both
│            predominantly hydrocarbon → COMPATIBLE
├── The plastic polymer network actually PROTECTS capsules by
│            distributing stress more evenly under traffic loading
└── VERDICT: Synergistic — no adverse interaction expected
```

### 5.2 Zone-Specific Formulation Tuning

#### ZONE A: Mombasa Hot Coast — Formulation "SA-Coast"

| Component | Specification | Rationale |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Base bitumen | Pen 60/70 (harder grade) | Higher stiffness to resist extreme heat rutting |
| LDPE/PP dosage | **7%** of binder weight | Maximum stiffening; raises softening point to 65–72°C |
| PP:LDPE ratio | 60:40 | Higher PP for greater high-temp stiffness |
| Microcapsule core | 70% WCO + 30% bagasse bio-oil (no castor) | Higher-viscosity core to prevent over-softening in heat |
| Microcapsule dosage | 3% of binder | Slightly lower — ample natural healing temp |
| Shell thickness | 3–4 µm (thicker) | Prevent heat-induced premature release |
| Target softening point | **>65°C** | Must exceed max pavement temp (70°C) |
| Anti-stripping agent | 0.5% hydrated lime | Coral rag aggregates need adhesion boost |

#### ZONE B: Nairobi Highland — Formulation "SA-Highland"

| Component | Specification | Rationale |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Base bitumen | Pen 80/100 (standard) | Balanced flexibility and stiffness |
| LDPE/PP dosage | **6%** of binder weight | Optimal balance; standard formulation |
| PP:LDPE ratio | 40:60 | Higher LDPE for flexibility at cooler nights |
| Microcapsule core | 60% WCO + 20% castor oil + 20% bio-oil | Castor oil improves low-temp healing (cool nights) |
| Microcapsule dosage | **4%** of binder | Higher — healing window shorter (4–6 hrs/day) |
| Shell thickness | 2–3 µm (standard) | Standard rupture sensitivity |
| Target softening point | **>58°C** | Adequate for 55°C max pavement temp |
| Special addition | **+2% crumb rubber** | Extra fatigue resistance for heavy Nairobi traffic |

#### ZONE C: Athi River Black Cotton Soil — Formulation "SA-Expansive"

| Component | Specification | Rationale |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Base bitumen | Pen 80/100 (standard) | Balanced properties |
| LDPE/PP dosage | **6%** of binder weight | Standard stiffening |
| PP:LDPE ratio | 50:50 | Balanced for thermal cycling stress |
| Microcapsule core | 50% WCO + 30% castor oil + 20% bio-oil | Maximum low-temp flexibility |
| Microcapsule dosage | **4%** of binder | Higher — subgrade movement creates extra micro-cracks |
| Shell thickness | 2–3 µm (standard) | Good crack sensitivity |
| Target softening point | **>60°C** | Moderate thermal protection |
| **Critical additions** | **Geogrid reinforcement at base course** | Resists longitudinal cracking from subgrade swell |
| | **SBS elastomer at 2%** of binder | Additional elastic recovery for swell-shrink cycling |
| | **1m soil replacement** (black cotton → murram) | Mandatory subgrade treatment |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Black Cotton Soil Caveat:** No binder formulation — however advanced — can solve black cotton soil problems alone. Smart Asphalt SA-Expansive MUST be combined with proper subgrade treatment (lime stabilisation at 4–6%, or full soil replacement to 1m depth). The enhanced binder provides superior crack resistance to accommodate residual subgrade movement, but it cannot prevent structural heave.

| Microplastic generation during pavement wear | Low | Unknown | Plastic is encapsulated within bitumen matrix — NOT exposed to surface abrasion; further study needed | **LOW-MEDIUM** |
