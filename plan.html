The plan

Prove it under heavy trucks, in Dandora. Then scale.

The pilot, the business model, the market and what we need.

Photo: road beside the Dandora dumping site. SuSanA Secretariat, CC BY 2.0
The pilot

Four stages, proof first.

Months 1–4

1 · Lab

Prove the capsules survive hot mixing, measure how much cracks heal, and test fumes and leaching, at a university laboratory.

Months 5–8

2 · Truck yard

Lay Smart Asphalt beside normal asphalt in a private truck-parking yard in Dandora, under daily heavy trucks.

From month 3

3 · Jobs

A sorting hub near the dumpsite employing six waste pickers to collect, sort and shred plastic film.

Month 12+

4 · Public road

With the yard data, a public road section with KURA or KeNHA approval.

At the truck yard we will track temperature, strain and cracking in both sections, and sample runoff and wear debris for micro- and nanoplastics.

The business model

We buy from Dandora and sell to road builders.

  1. Buy

    Sorted plastic film from Dandora’s waste pickers, above today’s price, and used cooking oil from eateries.

  2. Make

    Our healing additive, at a small processing hub near the dumpsite.

  3. Sell

    To asphalt plants and contractors working on KURA, KeNHA, KeRRA and county roads, priced per tonne of asphalt. Later, license the recipe to asphalt producers across the region.

Unit economics (our model): about KES 700 per tonne of asphalt, at a cost of about KES 545, a margin of about 22%. For the customer, the bitumen saved (about KES 750 per tonne) nearly pays for the additive. Source M3Source M7

The market

A KES 2.3 billion-a-year opportunity in East Africa.

TAM, East Africa: about 181,000 tonnes of road bitumen imported a year (USD 109 M). At KES 700 per tonne of asphalt, our additive’s potential is KES 2.3 bn a year. Source K1

SAM, Kenya: 40,000–88,000 tonnes of bitumen a year, about KES 760 M for our additive.

SOM, year 5: 5% of Kenya’s volume through Nairobi asphalt plants, about KES 38 M a year.

How we calculated it: bitumen tonnes ÷ 5.5% (bitumen share of asphalt) × KES 700. The bitumen data is official; the price and share are our model.
