The problem

Roads keep breaking, while the waste that could help prevent them burns in Dandora.

Photo: Nairobi road, August 2025. Brihaspati, CC BY-SA 4.0
1. Roads that fail early

Kenya keeps paying to fix the same roads.

239,000 kmof public roads, of which only about 24,000 km are paved (2024)Source P7
30% + 46%of the network in poor and fair condition (2023 survey)Source P10
15 → 5 yrsa road built for 15 years can fail in 5 under overloaded trucksSource P11
KES 115.6 bnproposed for road maintenance in 2025/26Source K3

Asphalt fails in a predictable way: the sun and air slowly harden the bitumen, heavy trucks bend the road thousands of times a day, a hairline crack opens, water gets in, and the crack grows into a pothole. Today we wait for the pothole, then patch it. A road that repaired small cracks early would last much longer.

2. An imported, fossil glue

Every tonne of road bitumen is imported.

Bitumen is a petroleum product, and Kenya makes none. UN customs data shows Kenya imported 50,592 tonnes (USD 27.5 million) in 2023 and 39,850 tonnes (USD 20.1 million) in 2024: about KES 2.6–3.9 billion a year. Source P1Source P2

That ties our roads to one region, one shipping route and the dollar. Using less imported bitumen, and needing fewer repairs, cuts both cost and carbon.
