What we have not proven

The honest limits of this project.

A good idea is not the same as a proven one. Here is exactly where Smart Asphalt stands.

StageLaboratory-validation concept (TRL 4 to 5): components are documented in research, but no Smart Asphalt sample has been made or tested by us, and there is no Kenyan road trial.
How much healingUncertain. Published results run from a few percentage points to about 80%, and we have not tested our own recipe. The numbers in our animation and simulator are illustrative estimates. Evidence T2
Capsules under trafficThe design intent is that capsules stay shut in everyday traffic and open at cracks. One published test saw more than 80% capsule rupture after repeated heavy loading, so this must be tested. Evidence T3
MicroplasticsWhether plastic-modified roads release microplastics is still being studied. Estimates suggest far less than tyre wear, but it is not settled. Evidence T7
Cost and lifetimeThe claims of about 1.5% higher build cost, 30-37% lower life-cycle cost, and a design life of 20-30 years are our own models. None is measured. Evidence M2Evidence M3
Competing uses of wasteBagasse already powers sugar mills, and cooking oil is already exported or turned into biodiesel. The waste is not all "free". Evidence W3Evidence W4
ScaleOne plant would use a tiny share of Kenya's waste (under 0.1% of its plastic). The goal is a national standard, not one factory.
Open inconsistenciesOur reports use different road widths and thicknesses, so per-kilometre material totals are not yet reconciled. We do not quote them. Evidence M8
Indirect costsWider costs of bitumen dependence (delays, risk premiums) are our own estimates and are not sourced. Evidence P4

What would change our mind

  • Laboratory tests showing that capsules add little healing at Kenyan temperatures.
  • Evidence that capsules break under normal heavy traffic, not only at cracks.
  • Plastic-modified samples that separate in storage or shed particles.
  • A cost model that, once measured, does not beat conventional asphalt over its life.

The next step

Laboratory tests of the binder and capsules, then three 500 metre pilot road sections beside conventional control sections, with sensors. We need a materials or chemistry laboratory partner and a road authority willing to host a pilot.
